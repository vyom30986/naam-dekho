How the conversion works, and where it loses

There are two stages. First the romanised name becomes Devanagari, using our own syllable engine — no free library does romanised-to-Devanagari acceptably, so we wrote one. Then Devanagari becomes Malayalam through the Brahmic-to-Brahmic conversion in @indic-transliteration/sanscript, which is accurate because the two scripts descend from the same system. Everything runs offline. There is no API behind it, which is why every name on this page could be rendered rather than a sample.

The second stage is the well-behaved one. The first is where the guessing lives, and it guesses because a romanised Indian name genuinely underdetermines its spelling. “Rita” is रीता for one family and ऋता for another, and nothing in the four letters says which. A trailing lone “a” is read as the long ā, because Priya is प्रिया far more often than प्रिय. A nasal before a stop is written as anusvara — संजय, not सन्जय — but not before y, r, l or v, which is why Ananya stays अनन्या. Each of those is a rule that is usually right and sometimes wrong.

Across 536 published names, 25 groups of romanised spellings arrive at one Devanagari spelling, and therefore at one Malayalam spelling. This happens before any script is chosen, so it is not something Malayalam did.

Questions people ask

How do I write my name in Malayalam?

The table on this page carries 120 published names already written out in Malayalam. If a name is not among them, the check on the home page runs the same two-stage conversion on any name you type: the romanised spelling becomes Devanagari through our own syllable engine, and the Devanagari becomes Malayalam. It runs offline and returns all ten Indian scripts at once, not just this one.

Is this the spelling a Malayalam speaker would use?

Every Devanagari letter these names use has its own letter in Malayalam, and no published name is dropped, so nothing is lost at that stage. What can still be wrong is the Devanagari itself — a romanised name does not fully determine its spelling, and the engine picks the likelier reading. Your family's spelling is the authority.

Which languages are written in the Malayalam script?

Malayalam (about 3.5 crore first-language speakers in India at the 2011 census, official in Kerala and Lakshadweep, and in Mahé). Declared a classical language of India in 2013.

Why does a name here look different from the way my family writes it?

Because the conversion starts from roman letters, and roman letters lose the distinctions Devanagari makes. “Rita” is रीता in one family and ऋता in another, and the spelling cannot tell you which. In this corpus 25 groups of romanised spellings arrive at the same Devanagari for exactly that reason. We publish the likelier reading and say so. For a certificate or an invitation, treat it as a draft your family corrects.
