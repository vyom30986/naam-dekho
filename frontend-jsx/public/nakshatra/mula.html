Why a pandit gives you a letter, not a name

At a namkaran the family is usually handed a syllable and left to find the name themselves. The pandit reads the Moon's position from the janam kundli, which fixes the birth nakshatra and then, more finely, which quarter of it the Moon occupied. Those quarters are the padas: 3°20′ of sky each, Mula's four running from 240°00′ – 253°20′. Each pada carries one syllable, and the child's name traditionally begins with it. Mula sits wholly inside Dhanu (Sagittarius), so all four padas share one moon sign.

The convention is old and practical. A syllable is a constraint the whole family can work inside — grandparents, parents and a priest can all agree on “start with Ye” long before anyone agrees on a name. It also keeps the name tied to the chart rather than to fashion, which is the point of the ceremony.

This page runs the table backwards, which is the direction that helps once you already have a name in mind: it lists published names that start with Mula's syllables. It cannot tell you a child's nakshatra — only the birth date, and ideally the birth time, can do that. Enter them on the name check and we calculate the Moon's actual position instead of reading the name.

Questions families ask

Which letter should a baby born under Mula nakshatra be named with?

Mula has four padas and each carries one syllable: pada 1 is Ye, pada 2 is Yo, pada 3 is Bha, pada 4 is Bhi. The pada the Moon was in at birth picks one of the four. A pada is 3°20′ of the ecliptic — roughly six hours of the Moon's motion — so without a recorded birth time the pada is often uncertain, and families then choose from all four syllables.

Which rashi does Mula nakshatra belong to?

Mula spans 240°00′ – 253°20′ of the sidereal zodiac. It sits wholly inside Dhanu (Sagittarius), so all four padas share one moon sign.

Does the name have to start with the pada syllable?

No. It is a convention, not a rule. The janam kundli is the authority for the birth star; the syllable is what the tradition derives from it, and plenty of families use it as a shortlist rather than a constraint. A name outside these four syllables is not an incorrect name.
