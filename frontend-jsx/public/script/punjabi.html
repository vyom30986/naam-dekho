What Gurmukhi cannot keep

Gurmukhi has a smaller consonant inventory than Devanagari, and one set of letters lands on a single letter here. This is the script working correctly, not a conversion fault — but it does mean a Gurmukhi spelling carries less information than the Devanagari it came from, and cannot always be read back.

How the conversion works, and where it loses

There are two stages. First the romanised name becomes Devanagari, using our own syllable engine — no free library does romanised-to-Devanagari acceptably, so we wrote one. Then Devanagari becomes Gurmukhi through the Brahmic-to-Brahmic conversion in @indic-transliteration/sanscript, which is accurate because the two scripts descend from the same system. Everything runs offline. There is no API behind it, which is why every name on this page could be rendered rather than a sample.

The second stage is the well-behaved one. The first is where the guessing lives, and it guesses because a romanised Indian name genuinely underdetermines its spelling. “Rita” is रीता for one family and ऋता for another, and nothing in the four letters says which. A trailing lone “a” is read as the long ā, because Priya is प्रिया far more often than प्रिय. A nasal before a stop is written as anusvara — संजय, not सन्जय — but not before y, r, l or v, which is why Ananya stays अनन्या. Each of those is a rule that is usually right and sometimes wrong.

Across 536 published names, 25 groups of romanised spellings arrive at one Devanagari spelling, and therefore at one Gurmukhi spelling. This happens before any script is chosen, so it is not something Gurmukhi did.

Questions people ask

How do I write my name in Punjabi?

The table on this page carries 120 published names already written out in Gurmukhi. If a name is not among them, the check on the home page runs the same two-stage conversion on any name you type: the romanised spelling becomes Devanagari through our own syllable engine, and the Devanagari becomes Gurmukhi. It runs offline and returns all ten Indian scripts at once, not just this one.

Is this the spelling a Punjabi speaker would use?

Usually, with the caveats this page is explicit about. Gurmukhi writes श and ष as ਸ਼. A spelling here therefore carries less information than the Devanagari behind it. ऋ has no Gurmukhi letter at all, and comes out spelled as ਰਿ. Where the conversion cannot represent a sound at all we drop the rendering rather than print broken text.

Which languages are written in the Gurmukhi script?

Punjabi (about 3.3 crore first-language speakers in India at the 2011 census, official in Punjab). Gurmukhi is the script of the Guru Granth Sahib.

Why does a name here look different from the way my family writes it?

Because the conversion starts from roman letters, and roman letters lose the distinctions Devanagari makes. “Rita” is रीता in one family and ऋता in another, and the spelling cannot tell you which. In this corpus 25 groups of romanised spellings arrive at the same Devanagari for exactly that reason. We publish the likelier reading and say so. For a certificate or an invitation, treat it as a draft your family corrects.
