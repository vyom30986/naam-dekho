There is no row for 9. Chaldean treats it as sacred and gives it to no letter, so a 9 can only arrive through reduction. 16 of the 26 letters are valued differently by the two systems, which is why a name can read as one number here and another elsewhere.

How the number is worked out

Take every letter, look up its value, add, reduce. Anu, one of the published names:

A1 N5 U6

A(1) + N(5) + U(6) = 12

12 → 1 + 2 = 3

Anu therefore carries root 3 — number 3, ruled by ♃ Jupiter. Nothing else goes into it: not the birth date, not the gender, not the meaning. Spaces and punctuation are dropped, and the name is read as you write it.

What a root number is and is not

It is arithmetic over a fixed table. Every value on this page can be checked with a pen, and that is deliberate: a reading nobody can reproduce is an opinion with a number attached. Whether the tradition means anything is a separate question, and not one a name checker gets to answer for you.

What it is not is a verdict. In a live check the numerology tile can read favourable, neutral, or not traditionally favoured for the industry you type — and that last one lowers a score without ever becoming a conflict. The things that can genuinely stop a name are elsewhere in the report: a trademark already registered in your class, a company on the MCA register with your name, a domain someone else owns. We keep the cultural readings and the hard facts in different columns on purpose.
